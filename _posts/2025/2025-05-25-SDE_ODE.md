## 始於物理：流體力學 transport equation

> **The transport equation (or convection-diffusion equation) describes how a scalar quantity (like temperature, concentration, or momentum component) is transported within a fluid. Its general form includes terms for convection, diffusion, and sources or sinks:**

$$
\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial t}=\nabla \cdot(\mathbf{u} \Phi)-\nabla \cdot(\Gamma \nabla \Phi)+S
$$

where $\Phi$ is the scalar quantity, $\mathbf{u}$ is the velocity vector, $\Gamma$ is the diffusion coefficient, and $S$ is a source term.

我們進一步分析物理意義。先假設無源或無漏 $S=0$, $\Phi$ 基本是 conservative 的純量，例如濃度。
$$
\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial t}=\nabla \cdot(\mathbf{u} \Phi)-\nabla \cdot(\Gamma \nabla \Phi) = - \nabla \cdot(-\mathbf{u} \Phi +\Gamma \nabla \Phi) 
$$
左手是純量時間變化量 = 右手是空間的散出（或吸入）量。分成兩項：一個是外部場造成 flow 的 drift item, 另一個是因爲濃度差 (隨機) 散出的 diffusion item.  如果 $\Phi$ 隨時間減少 (負值)，散度是正值，所以需要一個負號。$\mathbf{u}$ 的正向定義是向内，可以和 local 負號抵消。

在半導體的 transport equation 在平衡態 drift 和 diffusion 互相抵消。所以兩者的符號相反。

在電磁學和 flow matching 一般 flow 定義的方向相反，所以是

$$
\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial t}=\nabla \cdot(\mathbf{u} \Phi)-\nabla \cdot(\Gamma \nabla \Phi) = - \nabla \cdot(\mathbf{u} \Phi +\Gamma \nabla \Phi) 
$$

### 巨觀 vs 微觀，PDE/ODE vs SDE，$\boldsymbol{x}_t$ vs $p(x, t)$ 

### Fokker-Planck 偏微分方程

在 generative AI 的 diffusion process 或是 flow method:  守恆量是機率 (任意時間點的機率和為 1) $\Phi = p(x, t)$，沒有 source $S=0$.   不過一般物理的 diffusion 是從高濃度向低濃度，但是 probability 卻是從低機率 diffuse 到高機率。可以等價視爲負 diffusion constant:  $D = -\Gamma$ ，如果假設是 isotropic diffusion, $D(t)$ 和位置無關，可以和時間有關 (noise scheduling)。Fokker-Planck equation 一般寫成：
$$
\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}=-\nabla \cdot[\mathbf{u}(x,t)\, p(x,t)]+\nabla \cdot[D(t) \nabla p(x,t)] = -\nabla \cdot[\mathbf{u}(x,t)\, p(x,t)]+D(t) \Delta  p(x,t)  
$$

另一個表示

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\log p(x,t)}{\partial t}&= \frac{1}{p(x,t)}\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}\\
&=  -\frac{\nabla \cdot[\mathbf{u}(x,t)\, p(x,t)]}{p(x,t)}+D(t) \frac{\Delta  p(x,t)}{p(x,t)}\\  
&=  -\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}(x,t)-\mathbf{u}(x,t)\cdot \nabla\log p(x,t) +D(t) \frac{\Delta  p(x,t)}{p(x,t)}\\  
&=  -\mathbf{u}(x,t)\cdot \nabla\log p(x,t) \underbrace{- \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}(x,t) +D(t)[\Delta \log p(x,t)] +  \| \nabla \log p(x,t) \|^2]}_{=\frac{d\log p(x,t)}{d t}}\\ 
\end{aligned}$$
### 幾個常用公式 check：

**Conservation of probability:**

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \int p(x,t)\, dx = \int \frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}\, dx  = 0$$
$$
\mathbb{E}_{p} \left[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \log p(x,t) \right] = \int p(x,t) \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \log p(x,t)\, dx = \int \frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}\, dx = 0
$$
**Score function 期望值 $= 0$**：$\mathbb{E}(\nabla \log p)=0$.   所有的 distribution (Appendix S), 不只是 Gaussian!

**Fisher information $\ge 0$**：$I(p) = \mathbb{E}(\|\nabla \log p\|^2)=\int p \|\nabla \log p\|^2 dx\, \ge 0$ .

**Entropy $> 0$：**$H(t) = - \mathbb{E}_p\left[\log p(x,t)\right] = -\int p(x,t)\log p(x,t)dx \,(> 0)$

**Entropy 時間變化：**
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{d H(t)}{dt} &= -\int \frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}\log p(x,t)dx \\
&= \int \left[\nabla \cdot[\boldsymbol{u}(x,t)\, p(x,t)]-D(t) \Delta  p(x,t)\right] \log p(x,t)dx \\
&= \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{u}(x,t)]}_{\text{Drift contribution}} + \underbrace{D(t) I(p)}_{\text{Diffusion contribution}},
\end{aligned}$$
- Diffusion only $\ge 0$
- Drift only 可能 > 0 (膨脹 flow),  < 0 (壓縮 flow),  = 0 (incompressible flow)

### 微觀 sample $x_t$  Ito SDE

Fokker-Planck equation 是以 ODE (主要是偏微分) 的形式，代表（巨觀）平均 flow $p(x,t)$ 的形式。另一條路線則是 Ito SDE，代表（微觀）**個別樣本隨機運動 $x_t$ 的形式。個別 sample 一定是 $t$ 的函數，因此是全微分表示法。**

Ito SDE general 的形式如下。這裏的 $\sigma(x_t, t)$ 是 "**incremental**" additive noise，我們之後會改成 $g(t)$. 因爲很容易和後面的 "**total**" additive Gaussian noise $\boldsymbol{x}_t = \boldsymbol{x}_0 + \sigma(t) \boldsymbol{z}_t$ 混淆！ 
$$
d \boldsymbol{x}_t = \mathbf{u}(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) d t+\sigma(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) d \boldsymbol{w}_t
$$
對應的 Fokker-Planck equation
$$
\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}= -\nabla \cdot[\mathbf{u}(x,t)\, p(x,t)]+ \Delta [D(x, t) p(x,t)]\quad \text{ where }D(x,t) = \frac{\sigma^2(x,t)}{2}  
$$

> [!驗證平均流場] 
> 
> 驗證一下 $\mathbf{u}$ 是平均流和 Fokker-Planck 一致： 
> $$\mathbf{u}(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) = \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{d \boldsymbol{x}_t}{dt}\right]$$


我們改成 isotropic 形式， 把 $\sigma(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t)$ 改成 $g(t)$，$\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x}_t,t)$ 變成 $\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_t,t)$. 
$$
d \boldsymbol{x}_t = \boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) d t+g(t) d \boldsymbol{w}_t\quad \text{ where }D(t) = \frac{g^2(t)}{2}
$$
對應的偏微分 PDE：
$$
\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}=-\nabla \cdot[\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)\, p(x,t)]+\frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta  p(x,t)  
$$
另一個表示法：
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\log p(x,t)}{\partial t}&= \frac{1}{p(x,t)}\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}\\
&=  -\mathbf{u}(x,t)\cdot \nabla\log p(x,t) \underbrace{- \nabla \cdot \mathbf{f}(x,t) +\frac{g^2(t)}{2}[\Delta \log p(x,t)] +  \| \nabla \log p(x,t) \|^2]}_{=\frac{d\log p(x,t)}{d t}}\\ 
\end{aligned}$$

儘量不要用全微分 ODE ! 
$$
\frac{d \log p(x,t)}{d t}=-\nabla \cdot\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)+\frac{g^2(t)}{2} [ \Delta  \log p(x,t) + \|\nabla\log p(x,t)\|^2]  
$$
除了在 flow based 或是 particle trajectory $x(t)$ 的性質，例如：

$$\log p(x_t)=\log p(x_0)+\int_0^t \frac{d \log p(x,t)}{d t}dt$$

Fokker-Planck ODE/PDE (flow) 和 Ito SDE (sample) 的關聯有很多應用:
- **Stochastic thermodynamics**: 微觀熱力學，非平衡動力學 (後文)
- **Generative modeling**: score-based models approximate $\nabla \log p$ or flow matching $\nabla\cdot f$ (另文).
- **Information geometry**: $\|\nabla \log p\|^2$ is the Fisher information density.
- **Schrödinger bridge (從一個 distribution 變成另一個 distribution)**: this term appears in the dynamic formulation of entropy-regularized optimal transport.


## Gaussian Process

上文 $p(x,t)$  可以是任何 distribution or process.  我們考慮最特別的情況就是 $p(x,t=0)=p(x_0)$ 是 Gaussian $\sim N(\mu(0), \sigma^2(0))$.

可以證明只要 Fokker-Planck equation 的 drift term 和 diffusion coefficient 是線性， 所有 $p(x,t)=p(x_t)$ 都是 Gaussian $\sim N(\mu(t), \sigma^2(t))$。這很直覺但是很重要：因為我們只需要追蹤 $\mu(t)$ 和 $\sigma^2(t)$ 的變化就可以。

假設 $p(\boldsymbol{x}_t) = \mathcal{N}(\mu(t), \sigma^2(t))$  with initial condition $\mu(0) = x_0$,  $\sigma^2(0)=0$ (或是非常小)

### 計算 $\mu(t)$ and $\sigma^2(t)$

我們從 Ito SDE 開始
$$
d \boldsymbol{x}_t = \boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) d t+g(t) d \boldsymbol{w}_t
$$
因爲要綫性，我們假設 $\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t)=-\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x}_t$,   OU process,  special case 是 $\beta(t)=0$
$$
d \boldsymbol{x}_t = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x}_t d t+g(t) d \boldsymbol{w}_t
$$
因爲 $\boldsymbol{x}_t\sim N(\mu(t), \sigma^2(t))$ ，因此 trace $\boldsymbol{x}_t = \mu(t) + \sigma(t) \boldsymbol{z}_t$


**Mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}(t)=\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$ 要滿足的微分方程:** 就是把 Ito SDE 兩端取期望值 $\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{x}_t] =\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$, $\mathbb{E}[d\boldsymbol{w}_t] =0$
$$
\frac{d\boldsymbol{\mu}_t}{dt} = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{\mu}_t
$$
$$
\quad \boldsymbol{\mu}(t) = \boldsymbol{x}_0 \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \int_0^t \beta(s) ds \right) = \sqrt{\alpha(t)} \boldsymbol{x}_0, \quad \text{ where }\alpha(t) = e^{-\int_0^t \beta(s) ds}
$$
$\alpha(0)=1$ and $\alpha(\infty) \to 0$

一個特例是:  $\beta(t)=\beta$ (constant),  $\alpha(t) = e^{-\beta t}$, $\alpha(t)$ exponential decay.  
實務上 $\beta(t)$ 用 linear decay, 或是 cosine scheduling.


**Variance  $\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2(t)$ 要滿足的微分方程 (Itô 引理)：

$$
d\left(\boldsymbol{x}_t^2\right) = 2 \boldsymbol{x}_t\, d\boldsymbol{x}_t + (d\boldsymbol{x}_t)^2
\Rightarrow
\frac{d}{dt} \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{x}_t^2] = 2 \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{x}_t\, \text{drift}] + \mathbb{E}[\text{diffusion}^2]
$$

$$
\frac{d{\sigma}^2(t)}{dt} = -\beta(t) {\sigma}^2(t) + g^2(t)
$$

解得：
$$
\sigma^2(t) = \int_0^t g^2(s) \exp\left(-\int_s^t \beta(r)\,dr\right) ds
$$
因此，
$$
\sigma^2(t) = \sigma^2(0) \exp\left(-\int_0^t \beta(r)\,dr\right) + \int_0^t g^2(s) \exp\left(-\int_s^t \beta(r)\,dr\right) ds
$$
利用 $\alpha(t) = e^{-\int_0^t \beta(s)ds}$，可表示为：
$$
    \boxed{
    \sigma^2(t)
    \;=\;
    \alpha(t)\left(\,\int_0^t\frac{g^2(s)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds + \sigma^2(0)\right)}.
$$
第二項 $\sigma^2(0)\alpha(t)$ 會 decay 到可以忽略。
假設 $\sigma^2(0) = 0$
$$
  \boxed{
    \sigma^2(t)
    \;=\;
    \alpha(t)\,\int_0^t\frac{g^2(s)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds .
  }
$$
特例：假設 $g^2(t)$ 和 $\beta(t)$ 是常數。$\sigma^2(t) = \frac{g^2}{\beta}(1-e^{-\beta t})$，最大值是 $\frac{g^2}{\beta}$

### 2. 計算 $\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}$

正態分布 $p(\boldsymbol{x}, t) = \mathcal{N}(\mu(t), \sigma^2(t)I)$ 的时间导数为：
$$
\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = p \left[ \frac{(\boldsymbol{x}-\mu) \dot{\mu}}{\sigma^2} + \frac{(\boldsymbol{x}-\mu)^2 - d\sigma^2}{2\sigma^4} \dot{\sigma^2} \right]
$$
代入線性條件 $\dot{\mu} = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)\mu$ 和 $\dot{\sigma^2} = -\beta(t)\sigma^2 + g^2(t)$，得到：
$$\begin{align}
\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} &= p \left[ -\frac{\beta(t)(\boldsymbol{x}-\mu)\mu}{2\sigma^2} + \frac{(\boldsymbol{x}-\mu)^2 - d\sigma^2}{2\sigma^4} \left(-\beta(t)\sigma^2 + g^2(t)\right) \right]\\
&= p \left[ -\beta(t)\frac{x(x-\mu) - d \sigma^2}{2\sigma^2} +\frac{(\boldsymbol{x}-\mu)^2 - d\sigma^2}{2\sigma^4} g^2(t) \right]\\
\end{align}$$

### 3. 計算 $\frac{\partial \log p}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}$

$$
\frac{\partial \log p}{\partial t} = \left[ \frac{(\boldsymbol{x}-\mu) \dot{\mu}}{\sigma^2} + \frac{(\boldsymbol{x}-\mu)^2 - d\sigma^2}{2\sigma^4} \dot{\sigma^2} \right]
$$
代入 $\dot{\mu}$ 和 $\dot{\sigma^2}$ 得：
$$
\frac{\partial \log p}{\partial t} = \left[ \underbrace{-\beta(t)\frac{x(x-\mu) - d \sigma^2}{2\sigma^2}}_{\text{drift}} +\underbrace{\frac{(\boldsymbol{x}-\mu)^2 - d\sigma^2}{2\sigma^4} g^2(t)}_{\text{diffusion}} \right]
$$

對比一下：
$$
\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}=-\nabla \cdot[\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)\, p(x,t)]+\frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta  p(x,t)  
$$
另一個表示法：
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\log p(x,t)}{\partial t}=  -\mathbf{u}(x,t)\cdot \nabla\log p(x,t) - \nabla \cdot \mathbf{f}(x,t) +\frac{g^2(t)}{2}[\Delta \log p(x,t)] +  \| \nabla \log p(x,t) \|^2]\\ 
\end{aligned}$$

- $\nabla \cdot[\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)\, p(x,t)] =  \left[ \beta(t)\frac{x(x-\mu) - d \sigma^2}{2\sigma^2}  \right] p$
- 更進一步分解：  $\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{f}(x,t) = -\frac{\beta(t)d}{2}$ and $\mathbf{u}(x,t)\cdot \nabla\log p(x,t) = \frac{\beta(t)x(x-\mu)}{2\sigma^2(t)}$
- Score function: $\nabla \log p = \frac{x-\mu}{\sigma^2}$ 
 - Fisher Information density function: $\|\nabla \log p\|^2 = \frac{\|x-\mu\|^2}{\sigma^4}$ 
 - $\Delta \log p = \frac{-d}{\sigma^2}$


## Examples:  VE, VP, Sub-VP

### VE: Diffusion only forward SDE

mean:  $\frac{d\boldsymbol{\mu}(t)}{dt} = \boldsymbol{f} = 0$,   $\to \boldsymbol{\mu}(t) = \mathbf{x}_0$

or $\beta(t)=0 \to \alpha(t)=1$  所以 $\boldsymbol{\mu}(t) = \mathbf{x}_0$

variance:  $\frac{d\boldsymbol{\sigma^2}(t)}{dt} = g^2(t)$$\to {\sigma^2}(t) = \int g^2(t)dt$
 $$p(\mathbf{x}, t) = N(\mathbf{x}_0, \int g^2(t) \,dt\,\mathbf{I}) = N(\mathbf{x}_0, \sigma^2(t)\,\mathbf{I})$$
 因此 trace $\boldsymbol{x}_t$  可以寫成
 $$\boxed{\mathbf{x}_t = \mathbf{x}_0 + \sigma(t) \mathbf{z}_t }$$ $\sigma(t) = \sqrt{\int g^2(t)dt}$,  可以很大 (variance explode)
 
Diffusion only Fokker-Planck equation 如下。**因爲 drift $u = 0$,  偏微分和全微分相等！**

$$
\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}= \frac{d p(x,t)}{d t} = \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta  p(x,t)  
$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\log p(x,t)}{\partial t}&= \frac{d \log p(x,t)}{d t}= \frac{g^2(t)}{2} [\Delta \log p(x,t)] +  \| \nabla \log p(x,t) \|^2]\\ 
\end{aligned}$$

 驗證上式滿足 Fokker-Planck PDE (Appendix H)
 $$
\boxed{
\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t} = \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta p(x,t) = \frac{\dot{\sigma}^2(t)}{2} \left( \frac{\|x-x_0\|^2}{\sigma^4(t)} - \frac{d}{\sigma^2(t)} \right) p = \dot{\sigma}^2(t) \left( \frac{\|x-x_0\|^2 - d \sigma^2(t)}{2\sigma^4(t)}  \right) p
}
$$
 where $d$  是 dimension. 

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\partial \log p(x,t)}{\partial t} =  \frac{g^2(t)}{2} [\Delta \log p(x,t)] +  \| \nabla \log p(x,t) \|^2] = \dot{\sigma}^2(t) \left( \frac{\|x-x_0\|^2 - d \sigma^2(t)}{2\sigma^4(t)}  \right)
}
$$
 - Score function: $\nabla \log p = \frac{x-x_0}{\sigma^2(t)}$ 
 - Fisher Information density function: $\|\nabla \log p\|^2 = \frac{\|x-x_0\|^2}{\sigma^4(t)}$ 
 - Density 積分後得到 Fisher Information:  $\frac{d}{\sigma^2(t)}$
$$
\mathcal{I}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[\|\nabla \log p(x)\|^2] = -\mathbb{E}[\Delta \log p(x)]
$$
 - Laplacian of log likelihood function 是常數 (spatial): $\Delta \log p = \frac{-d}{\sigma^2(t)}$, 滿足
$$
\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla \log p(x)\|^2] = -\mathbb{E}[\Delta \log p(x)] = \frac{d}{\sigma^2(t)}
$$

驗證 diffusion process 的 probability conservation。(即使有 drift 也是滿足 probability conservation, 而且 diffusion 和 drift 會各自滿足)
$$
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial \log p}{\partial t}\right] = \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{g^2(t)}{2} \left(\|\nabla \log p\|^2 +\Delta \log p  \right)\right]=0
$$
Diffusion 的 entropy 永遠增加。 $\sigma^2(t) = \int g^2(t)$ 是遞增函數。
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{d H(t)}{dt} &= -\int \frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}\log p(x,t)dx \\
&= \underbrace{\frac{g^2(t)}{2} I(p)}_{\text{Diffusion contribution}} = \frac{d}{2}\frac{d \log \sigma^2(t)}{d t} > 0
\end{aligned}$$
第一個 $d$ 是 dimension.

### OU process

$$
d\boldsymbol{x}_t = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x}_t \,dt + g(t)\, d\boldsymbol{w}_t
$$

This defines an OU process with time-dependent coefficients.

### Fokker–Planck Equation

The corresponding PDE is:

$$
\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x} p \right) + \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta p
$$

Trace $\boldsymbol{x}_t = \mu(t) + \sigma(t) \boldsymbol{z}_t$

**Mean 要滿足的微分方程:**
$$
\frac{d\boldsymbol{\mu}(t)}{dt} = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{\mu}(t)
$$
$$
\quad \boldsymbol{\mu}(t) = \boldsymbol{x}_0 \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \int_0^t \beta(s) ds \right) = \sqrt{\alpha(t)} \boldsymbol{x}_0, \quad \text{ where }\alpha(t) = e^{-\int_0^t \beta(s) ds}
$$
$\alpha(0)=1$ and $\alpha(\infty) \to 0$

**Variance 要滿足的微分方程: assuming $\sigma(0)=0$**
$$
\frac{d{\sigma}^2(t)}{dt} = -\beta(t) {\sigma}^2(t) + g^2(t)
$$
$$
  \boxed{
    \sigma^2(t)
    \;=\;
    \alpha(t)\,\int_0^t\frac{g^2(s)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds.
  }
$$

不同的 noise scheduling $g(t)$ 對應不同的方法：

#### VP - SDE:   $g(t) = \sqrt{\beta(t)}$
$$
d\boldsymbol{x}_t = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x}_t \,dt + \sqrt{\beta(t)}\, d\boldsymbol{w}_t
$$
$$
g(t) = \sqrt{\beta(t)} \quad\Rightarrow\quad g^2(t) = \beta(t)
$$

Recall the formula for variance of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with time-dependent coefficients:

$$
\sigma^2(t)
= \alpha(t) \int_0^t \frac{g^2(s)}{\alpha(s)}\, ds
\quad\text{where}\quad
\alpha(t) = e^{-\int_0^t \beta(s)\, ds}
$$

Substituting $g^2(s) = \beta(s)$ into the formula:

$$
\sigma^2(t)
= \alpha(t) \int_0^t \frac{\beta(s)}{\alpha(s)}\, ds
$$

Now notice that:

$$
\alpha'(s) = -\beta(s)\alpha(s)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\frac{\beta(s)}{\alpha(s)} = \frac{d}{ds}\left( \frac{1}{\alpha(s)} \right)
$$

So:

$$
\int_0^t \frac{\beta(s)}{\alpha(s)}\, ds
= \int_0^t \frac{d}{ds}\left( \frac{1}{\alpha(s)} \right) ds
= \left[ \frac{1}{\alpha(s)} \right]_0^t
= \left( \frac{1}{\alpha(t)} - \frac{1}{\alpha(0)} \right)
= \frac{1}{\alpha(t)}-1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sigma^2(t)
= \alpha(t) \left( \frac{1}{\alpha(t)} -1\right)
= 1-\alpha(t)
$$
所以最後的 $\boxed{\boldsymbol{x}_t = \sqrt{\alpha(t)}\mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1-\alpha(t)} \boldsymbol{z}_t}$

$\alpha(t) = e^{-\int_0^t \beta(s) ds}$;  即使 $\beta(t)$ 大到無窮大，還是會被 drift term 壓制，所以 $\sigma^2(t) < 1$ 



#### Sub-VP by Song  $g(t) = \sqrt{\beta(t)(1-\alpha^2(t))}$,  Appendix L

$$
g^2(t) = \beta(t)\left(1 - \alpha^2(t)\right)
$$
$$
d\boldsymbol{x}_t = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x}_t \,dt + \sqrt{\beta(t)(1-\alpha^2(t))}\, d\boldsymbol{w}_t
$$

## 正向 vs 反向

**結論：如果沒有 random 項，微分方程是 time reversible， 只是時間變負號** 
$p(x,t), \frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}, \frac{d \log p(x(t),t)}{dt}$:  正向和反向 PDE, ODE 都一樣，只有 time reverse $dt \to -dt$

但是 $x_t$ 正向和反向 Ito SDE 雖然都是 drift + diffusion (random),  但是反向要加上 score function 引起的 drift 修正項。

### Ito SDE 反向 $\ne$  正向 

**时间反转**：定义反向过程$\boldsymbol{y}_s = \boldsymbol{x}_{T - s}$，其中$s$是反向时间变量。根据扩散过程的时间反转定理，反向过程的漂移项需要调整以补偿原过程的漂移和扩散项的影响。

**调整漂移项**：反向过程的漂移项在原漂移项的负数基础上，加上由概率密度梯度 (i.e. score function) 引起的修正项。这个修正项的形式为$g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t)$，其中$D(t) = \frac{g(t)^2}{2}$。

**扩散项保持不变**：反向SDE的扩散项与原SDE相同，仍为$g(t) d\boldsymbol{w}_t$。

反向SDE的形式为：(此處 $t$ 遞減)
$$
d\boldsymbol{x}_t = \left[ \boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) \right] dt + g(t) d\boldsymbol{w}_t
$$

### ODE 反向  =  正向 (DDIM)

**概率流连续性方程**：ODE的 drift 项需要确保其概率演化与原SDE的Fokker-Planck方程一致。通过对比连续性方程和Fokker-Planck方程，得到 drift 项的调整形式。

**调整 drift 项**：ODE的 drift 项在原 drift 项基础上，减去$\frac{g(t)^2}{2} \nabla_x \log p(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t)$，以移除随机性的影响。

对应ODE的形式为：
$$
d\boldsymbol{x}_t = \left[ \boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) - \frac{g(t)^2}{2} \nabla_x \log p(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) \right] dt
$$

###  Fokker-Planck 反向 = 正向 (Appendix G)

在正确构造反向随机微分方程（SDE）的情况下，**反向过程的边际概率密度函数（marginal PDF）与正向过程在对应的时间点上是相同的**。具体来说，若正向过程的时间变量为 $t \in [0, T]$，则反向过程在时间 $s = T - t$ 的边际 PDF 等于正向过程在时间 $t$ 的边际 PDF，即：

$$
p_{\text{reverse}}(x, s) = p_{\text{forward}}(x, T - s).
$$

反向 Fokker-Planck 方程为：
$$
\boxed{
\frac{\partial p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t)}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \left[ \boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}, t) p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t) \right] - \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t)
}
$$

在时间反转 $s = T - t$ 下，其形式与正向 Fokker-Planck 方程一致，从而保证边际概率密度对称：
$$
p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, T - t) = p_{\text{forward}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t).
$$
**这一对称性成立的条件**：
   - 反向 SDE 的漂移项包含修正项 $g^2(t) \nabla_x \log p(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t)$，确保概率流的连续性。
   - 初始条件对称：反向过程在 $s = 0$（对应 $t = T$）的分布与正向过程在 $t = T$ 的分布相同。

**直观解释**  
   - **扩散模型中的例子**：在去噪扩散模型中，正向过程逐渐添加噪声，反向过程逐步去噪。若反向过程的漂移项修正正确（包含分数函数 $\nabla_x \log p$），则每个时间点的边际分布与正向过程对称。

$$

d \boldsymbol{x}_t = \left[-\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x}_t + g^2(t) \frac{x_t-\mu(t)}{\sigma^2(t)}\right] d t+g(t) d \boldsymbol{w}_t

因爲 $\boldsymbol{x}_t\sim N(\mu(t), \sigma^2(t))$ ，因此 trace $\boldsymbol{x}_t = \mu(t) + \sigma(t) \boldsymbol{z}_t$

**Mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}(t)=\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$ 要滿足的微分方程: 

把 Ito SDE 兩端取期望值 $\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{x}_t] =\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$, $\mathbb{E}[d\boldsymbol{w}_t] =0$, 
重點是 $\mathbb{E}[\nabla_x \log p(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t)]=0$, 所以**反向的 mean 從 $t$ 的角度和正向完全一樣！但如果從 $\tau=T-t$ 的角度則差了一個負號。**
$$
\frac{d\boldsymbol{\mu}_t}{dt} = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{\mu}_t
$$
$$
\quad \boldsymbol{\mu}(t) = \boldsymbol{x}_0 \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \int_0^t \beta(s) ds \right) = \sqrt{\alpha(t)} \boldsymbol{x}_0, \quad \text{ where }\alpha(t) = e^{-\int_0^t \beta(s) ds}
$$
$\alpha(0)=1$ and $\alpha(\infty) \to 0$.

d\left(\boldsymbol{x}_t^2\right) = 2 \boldsymbol{x}_t\, d\boldsymbol{x}_t + (d\boldsymbol{x}_t)^2

**正確的：應該用** $\tau = T - t$,

d \boldsymbol{x}_{\tau} = \left[-\frac{1}{2}\beta(T-\tau) \boldsymbol{x}_{\tau} + g^2(T-\tau) \frac{x_{\tau}-\mu_{\tau}}{\sigma^2_{\tau}}\right] (-d \tau)+g(T-\tau) d \boldsymbol{w}_{\tau}

$$
\frac{d\boldsymbol{\mu}_r}{d\tau} = -\frac{d\boldsymbol{\mu}}{dt}\bigg|_{t=T-\tau}, \quad \frac{d\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2_r}{d\tau} = -\frac{d\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2}{dt}\bigg|_{t=T-\tau}

### OU Process 反向 SDE

前面已經證明 mean and variance 的 ODE 微分方程是 reversible.  理論上 ODE 微分方程是 1-to-1，不過我們還是不厭其煩再把 mean and variance 直接算出來。

**反向的 mean:**
$$\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}(\tau) &= \boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}(\tau=0) \exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\tau}\beta(T-s)ds\right)\\
&= \boldsymbol{\mu}(T) \underbrace{\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\tau}\beta(T-s)ds\right)}_{\text{exponential growth}}\\
&= \boldsymbol{\mu}(T) \exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{T-t}^{T}\beta(s)ds\right) \\
&= \boldsymbol{\mu}(T) \exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\beta(s)ds-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)ds\right]\right) \\
&= \boldsymbol{\mu}(T) \sqrt{\frac{\alpha(t)}{\alpha(T)}} \\
&= \sqrt{\alpha(t)} \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}(T)}{\sqrt{\alpha(T)}}=\boldsymbol{\mu}(t) \\
\end{aligned}$$
注意 $\alpha(t)$ 是 exponential decay function, 但是 $\frac{\alpha(t)}{\alpha(T)}$ 卻是 exponential growth function.
這裏利用了 forward path 的公式：
$$
\quad \boldsymbol{\mu}(t) = \boldsymbol{x}_0 \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \int_0^t \beta(s) ds \right) = \sqrt{\alpha(t)} \boldsymbol{x}_0, \quad \text{ where }\alpha(t) = e^{-\int_0^t \beta(s) ds}
$$

**反向 variance:**
$$\begin{aligned}
    \sigma_r^2(\tau)
    \;&=\;
    \alpha(T-\tau)\,\int_0^{\tau}\frac{g^2(T-s)}{\alpha(T-s)}\,ds + \alpha(T-\tau)\sigma_r^2(\tau=0)\\
    \;=&\; \frac{\alpha(t)}{\alpha(T)}\,\int_{T-t}^{T}\frac{-g^2(s)\alpha(T)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds + \frac{\alpha(t)\sigma^2(T)}{\alpha(T)}\\
    \;=&\; \frac{\alpha(t)}{\alpha(T)}\,\left[\int_{T-t}^{T}\frac{-g^2(s)\alpha(T)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds + \sigma^2(T)\right]\\
     \;=&\; \frac{\alpha(t)}{\alpha(T)}\,\left[\int_{0}^{T}\frac{-g^2(s)\alpha(T)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds + \int_{0}^{t}\frac{g^2(s)\alpha(T)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds+ \sigma^2(T)\right]\\
	  \;=&\; \frac{\alpha(t)}{\alpha(T)}\,\left[-\alpha(T)\int_{0}^{T}\frac{g^2(s)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds + \alpha(T)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{g^2(s)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds+ \alpha(T)\,\int_0^T\frac{g^2(s)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds + \alpha(T)\sigma^2(0)\right]\\
	  \;=&\; \alpha(t)\,\left[ \int_{0}^{t}\frac{g^2(s)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds+ \sigma^2(0)\right] = \sigma^2(t)\\
\end{aligned}$$
此處用到 forward path 的公式：
$$
    \sigma^2(T)
    \;=\;
    \alpha(T)\,\int_0^T\frac{g^2(s)}{\alpha(s)}\,ds + \alpha(T)\sigma^2(0)
$$
$\alpha(t) = \exp({-\int_0^t \beta(s) ds})$
$\alpha(T-\tau) = \exp({\int_0^{\tau} \beta(T-s) ds})=\frac{\alpha(t)}{\alpha(T)}$

### 最终答案



Excellent question — and you're now honing in on a very clean and insightful formulation. Let's unpack it carefully.

You're proposing:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \log p(x(t), t)
= -\nabla \cdot f + \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla \cdot \nabla \log p = -\nabla \cdot f + \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla^2 \log p
$$

This is **almost correct**, and it actually *is* correct under a specific assumption: when you treat the total derivative deterministically (ignoring Itô correction terms that come from stochastic calculus). Let's see why, and when it's valid or not.

---

## 🧮 Two Ways to Think About the Total Derivative

### ✅ 1. **Expected (mean-field) evolution** — what the **density** evolves like

If you're **tracking how $\log p(x(t), t)$ evolves on average**, then using the **Fokker–Planck equation** and applying the chain rule gives:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \log p(x(t), t)
= \partial_t \log p + f \cdot \nabla \log p + \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \left( \nabla^2 \log p + \|\nabla \log p\|^2 \right)
$$

> This is the **Itô formula** applied to $\log p$.
> The term $\|\nabla \log p\|^2$ arises due to the nonlinear transformation (log of a stochastic function).

---

### 🚫 2. What you're proposing:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \log p = -\nabla \cdot f + \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla^2 \log p
$$

This is actually the **Eulerian time derivative** of $\log p$, derived directly from the **Fokker–Planck equation** (dividing both sides by $p$).
But **this is not the same** as the **Itô total derivative along a trajectory**.

Let me show it explicitly:

---

## 📘 Derivation from Fokker–Planck:

Start from:

$$
\partial_t p = -\nabla \cdot (f p) + \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla^2 p
$$

Divide both sides by $p$:

$$
\partial_t \log p = -\nabla \cdot f - f \cdot \nabla \log p + \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \left( \nabla^2 \log p + \|\nabla \log p\|^2 \right)
$$

Now take the **total derivative**:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \log p = \partial_t \log p + \dot{x} \cdot \nabla \log p
$$

Plug in $\dot{x} = f + \text{noise}$. The expected value (ignoring stochastic term) is:

$$
f \cdot \nabla \log p
$$

So when you add $f \cdot \nabla \log p$ to the expression above, the two $f \cdot \nabla \log p$ terms cancel:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt} \log p = -\nabla \cdot f + \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \left( \nabla^2 \log p + \|\nabla \log p\|^2 \right)
}
$$

So the correct form **must** include the $\|\nabla \log p\|^2$ term due to the **Itô correction**.

---

## ✅ Conclusion

So to answer directly:

> **Why not**

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \log p = -\nabla \cdot f + \frac{1}{2} g^2 \nabla^2 \log p?
$$

Because it **misses the nonlinear Itô term** $\frac{1}{2} g^2 \|\nabla \log p\|^2$, which is **required** when taking the total derivative of a nonlinear function of a stochastic process (like $\log p$).

This extra term comes from:

$$
\frac{1}{2} \text{Tr}(g^2 H_{\log p}) + \frac{1}{2} \| g \nabla \log p \|^2
$$

If you're okay with expectation-level or weak approximations, your version might suffice. But for **pathwise correctness**, you need the full Itô expression.

## Appendix G

要推导反向随机微分方程（SDE）对应的 Fokker-Planck 方程，我们从修改后的漂移项 $\boldsymbol{f}' = -\boldsymbol{f} + g^2(t) \nabla_x \log p(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t)$ 出发，并结合扩散项 $g(t)$。以下是详细推导步骤：

---

### **1. 正向 SDE 的 Fokker-Planck 方程**
正向 SDE 为：
$$
d\boldsymbol{x}_t = \boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) dt + g(t) d\boldsymbol{w}_t,
$$
其对应的 Fokker-Planck 方程为：
$$
\frac{\partial p(\boldsymbol{x}, t)}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot \left[ \boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}, t) p(\boldsymbol{x}, t) \right] + \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta p(\boldsymbol{x}, t),
$$
其中 $D(t) = \frac{g^2(t)}{2}$。

---

### **2. 反向 SDE 的构造**
反向 SDE 的漂移项被修正为：
$$
\boldsymbol{f}' = -\boldsymbol{f} + g^2(t) \nabla_x \log p(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t),
$$
因此反向 SDE 的形式为：
$$
d\boldsymbol{x}_t = \left[ -\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) + g^2(t) \nabla_x \log p(\boldsymbol{x}_t, t) \right] dt + g(t) d\boldsymbol{w}_t.
$$

---

### **3. 反向 Fokker-Planck 方程的推导**
反向过程的 Fokker-Planck 方程由以下公式给出：
$$
\frac{\partial p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t)}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot \left[ \boldsymbol{f}'(\boldsymbol{x}, t) p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t) \right] + \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t).
$$

将 $\boldsymbol{f}' = -\boldsymbol{f} + g^2(t) \nabla_x \log p$ 代入：
$$
\frac{\partial p_{\text{reverse}}}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot \left[ \left( -\boldsymbol{f} + g^2 \nabla_x \log p \right) p_{\text{reverse}} \right] + \frac{g^2}{2} \Delta p_{\text{reverse}}.
$$

展开括号内的项：
$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla \cdot \left[ \left( -\boldsymbol{f} + g^2 \nabla_x \log p \right) p_{\text{reverse}} \right] 
&= -\nabla \cdot \left[ \boldsymbol{f} p_{\text{reverse}} \right] + g^2 \nabla \cdot \left[ (\nabla_x \log p) p_{\text{reverse}} \right].
\end{aligned}
$$

注意到 $\nabla_x \log p = \frac{\nabla_x p}{p}$，因此：
$$
(\nabla_x \log p) p_{\text{reverse}} = \nabla_x p \quad \text{（当边际分布相同，即 $p_{\text{reverse}} = p$}.
$$

代入后得到：
$$
g^2 \nabla \cdot \left[ \nabla_x p \right] = g^2 \Delta p.
$$

因此，反向 Fokker-Planck 方程化简为：
$$
\frac{\partial p_{\text{reverse}}}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \left[ \boldsymbol{f} p_{\text{reverse}} \right] - g^2 \Delta p_{\text{reverse}} + \frac{g^2}{2} \Delta p_{\text{reverse}}.
$$

合并扩散项：
$$
\frac{\partial p_{\text{reverse}}}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \left[ \boldsymbol{f} p_{\text{reverse}} \right] - \frac{g^2}{2} \Delta p_{\text{reverse}}.
$$

---

### **4. 时间反转对称性**
为了与正向过程对应，定义反向时间 $s = T - t$，则时间导数的符号改变：
$$
\frac{\partial p_{\text{reverse}}}{\partial s} = -\frac{\partial p_{\text{reverse}}}{\partial t}.
$$

代入反向方程：
$$
\frac{\partial p_{\text{reverse}}}{\partial s} = -\nabla \cdot \left[ \boldsymbol{f} p_{\text{reverse}} \right] + \frac{g^2}{2} \Delta p_{\text{reverse}}.
$$

**这与正向 Fokker-Planck 方程形式一致**，即：
$$
\frac{\partial p_{\text{forward}}}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot \left[ \boldsymbol{f} p_{\text{forward}} \right] + \frac{g^2}{2} \Delta p_{\text{forward}}.
$$

因此，在反向时间 $s$ 下，反向过程的概率密度演化与正向过程对称。

---

### **5. 结论**
反向 Fokker-Planck 方程为：
$$
\boxed{
\frac{\partial p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t)}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \left[ \boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}, t) p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t) \right] - \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t)
}
$$

在时间反转 $s = T - t$ 下，其形式与正向 Fokker-Planck 方程一致，从而保证边际概率密度对称：
$$
p_{\text{reverse}}(\boldsymbol{x}, T - t) = p_{\text{forward}}(\boldsymbol{x}, t).
$$

## Appendix J: Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) Process

 Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) Process

We now add drift:

$$
d\boldsymbol{x}_t = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x}_t \,dt + g(t)\, d\boldsymbol{w}_t
$$

This defines an OU process with time-dependent coefficients.

### Fokker–Planck Equation

The corresponding PDE is:

$$
\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x} p \right) + \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta p
$$

Expanding the divergence term:

$$
\nabla \cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x} p \right) = \frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \left( d p + \boldsymbol{x} \cdot \nabla p \right)
$$

Let $p(x,t) = \mathcal{N}(\mu(t), \alpha^2(t)) = \frac{1}{(2\pi \alpha^2(t))^{d/2}} \exp\left(-\frac{\|x - \mu(t)\|^2}{2\alpha^2(t)}\right)$, where:

$$
\alpha^2(t) = \int_0^t \exp\left(-\int_s^t \beta(r) dr \right)^2 g^2(s)\, ds,
\quad \mu(0) = x_0,
\quad \mu(t) = x_0 \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \int_0^t \beta(s) ds \right).
$$

Then:

$$
\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = p(x,t) \left[ -\frac{d}{2} \cdot \frac{\dot{\alpha}^2(t)}{\alpha^2(t)} + \frac{\|x - \mu(t)\|^2}{2} \cdot \frac{\dot{\alpha}^2(t)}{\alpha^4(t)} + \frac{(x - \mu(t))^\top \dot{\mu}(t)}{\alpha^2(t)} \right]
$$

Also:

$$
\Delta p = \left( \frac{\|x - \mu(t)\|^2}{\alpha^4(t)} - \frac{d}{\alpha^2(t)} \right) p(x,t), \\
\nabla p = -\frac{x - \mu(t)}{\alpha^2(t)} p(x,t), \\
\boldsymbol{x} \cdot \nabla p = -\frac{\|x - \mu(t)\|^2}{\alpha^2(t)} p(x,t)
$$

Then the divergence term becomes:

$$
\nabla \cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) \boldsymbol{x} p \right) = \frac{1}{2} \beta(t) \left( d - \frac{\|x - \mu(t)\|^2}{\alpha^2(t)} \right) p(x,t)
$$

Putting it together:

$$
\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{2} \beta(t) \left( d - \frac{\|x - \mu(t)\|^2}{\alpha^2(t)} \right) p(x,t) + \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \left( \frac{\|x - \mu(t)\|^2}{\alpha^4(t)} - \frac{d}{\alpha^2(t)} \right) p(x,t)
$$

Using the definition of $\dot{\alpha}^2(t)$ and $\dot{\mu}(t)$, this matches $\partial_t p$.

Let $p(x,t) = \mathcal{N}(\mu(t), \alpha^2(t))$. Then:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \log p = -\frac{d}{2} \cdot \frac{\dot{\alpha}^2(t)}{\alpha^2(t)} + \frac{\|x - \mu(t)\|^2}{2} \cdot \frac{\dot{\alpha}^2(t)}{\alpha^4(t)} + \frac{(x - \mu(t))^\top \dot{\mu}(t)}{\alpha^2(t)}
$$

On the other hand:

$$
\nabla \log p = -\frac{x - \mu(t)}{\alpha^2(t)},
\quad \Delta \log p = -\frac{d}{\alpha^2(t)},
\quad \|\nabla \log p\|^2 = \frac{\|x - \mu(t)\|^2}{\alpha^4(t)}
$$

Hence:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \log p = -\nabla \cdot f(x,t) + \frac{g^2(t)}{2} \left[ \Delta \log p + \|\nabla \log p\|^2 \right]
$$

with $f(x,t) = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) x$ and $\nabla \cdot f = -\frac{d}{2}\beta(t)$.

### Conclusion

Both pure diffusion and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes with Gaussian solutions satisfy their respective Fokker–Planck equations and the log-probability total derivative (Lagrangian) formulation. This provides a strong analytical foundation for diffusion modeling, score-based generative models, and reverse-time SDE analysis.

## Appendix N

Great question. Let's analyze the sign of:

$$
\frac{dH(t)}{dt} = \mathbb{E}[\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{f}(x,t)] + \frac{g^2(t)}{2} I(p)
$$

This tells us how the **differential entropy** $H(t) = -\int p(x,t) \log p(x,t) dx$ evolves over time, for a probability distribution $p(x,t)$ governed by a Fokker–Planck equation with drift $\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)$ and scalar diffusion coefficient $g(t)$.

---

### **Term-by-term analysis**

#### **1. Diffusion term: $\frac{g^2(t)}{2} I(p)$**

* Always **non-negative**, since $g^2(t) \ge 0$ and Fisher information $I(p) \ge 0$.
* Represents the **entropy-increasing** effect of diffusion (spreading out the distribution).
* Strictly positive unless $p$ is uniform or a Dirac delta (infinite entropy case).

#### **2. Drift term: $\mathbb{E}[\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{f}(x,t)]$**

* Can be **positive, negative, or zero**, depending on the vector field $\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)$.
* If $\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{f} < 0$ (e.g., a contracting flow), this term decreases entropy.
* If $\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{f} > 0$ (e.g., expanding flow), this term increases entropy.

---

### **Overall sign of $\frac{dH}{dt}$?**

We **cannot assert the sign of $\frac{dH}{dt}$ in general**, because it depends on the **balance between drift and diffusion**:

* If **diffusion dominates** (large $g(t)$, or small $\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{f}$), entropy increases: $\frac{dH}{dt} > 0$
* If **drift dominates**, and especially if it's compressive: $\frac{dH}{dt} < 0$
* If they balance: $\frac{dH}{dt} = 0$, which can happen in stationary cases

---

### **Special case: Pure diffusion (no drift)**

If $\boldsymbol{f}(x,t) = 0$, then:

$$
\frac{dH(t)}{dt} = \frac{g^2(t)}{2} I(p) \ge 0
$$

So **entropy always increases** — this is **consistent with the heat equation**, where a peaked distribution spreads out over time.

---

### **Special case: Deterministic flow (no diffusion)**

If $g(t) = 0$, then:

$$
\frac{dH(t)}{dt} = \mathbb{E}[\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{f}(x,t)]
$$

This can be positive or negative depending on whether the deterministic flow expands or contracts space.

---

### **Conclusion**

We **cannot generally say** whether $\frac{dH}{dt}$ is positive or negative **without knowing more about the drift and diffusion**. However:

* **Diffusion always increases entropy**
* **Drift can increase or decrease entropy**, depending on whether it compresses or expands probability mass.

This interplay is fundamental in stochastic processes and nonequilibrium thermodynamics.

### Why not the material derivative $\frac{d p}{dt}$?

The **material derivative** $\frac{d p}{dt} = \frac{\partial p}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla p$ describes how $p$ changes **along a moving particle's path** in a flow field $\mathbf{u}(x,t)$. It's used in **fluid mechanics** or **stochastic processes** when tracking individual trajectories.

However, **entropy** is a **global functional** of the probability density:

$$
H(t) = -\int p(x,t) \log p(x,t) \, dx
$$

So when you take its time derivative, you apply the **chain rule** to the integrand:

$$
\frac{dH}{dt} = -\int \left( \frac{\partial p}{\partial t} \log p + \frac{\partial p}{\partial t} \right) dx
$$

But since $\int \frac{\partial p}{\partial t} dx = \frac{d}{dt} \int p(x,t) dx = 0$, that second term vanishes.

So you're left with:

$$
\frac{dH}{dt} = -\int \frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t} \log p(x,t) \, dx
$$

### ✅ Therefore:

Use the **partial derivative $\partial p / \partial t$**, **not** the material derivative $d p / dt$.

---

### 檢查如果用全微分錯誤的答案

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{d H(t)}{dt} &= -\int \left[\frac{d p(x,t)}{d t}\log p(x,t) + p(x,t) \frac{1}{p(x,t)}\frac{d p(x,t)}{d t}\right]dx\\
&= -\int \frac{d p(x,t)}{d t}\log p(x,t)dx - \int \frac{d p(x,t)}{d t}dx\\
&= -\int \frac{d p(x,t)}{d t}\log p(x,t)dx -  \frac{d}{d t}\underbrace{\int p(x,t) dx}_{=1}\\
&= -\int \frac{d p(x,t)}{d t}\log p(x,t)dx \\
&= \int \left[\nabla \cdot[\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)]\, p(x,t)-\frac{g^2(t)}{2} \Delta  p(x,t)\right] \log p(x,t)dx \\
&= \underbrace{\int\nabla \cdot[\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)]\, p(x,t)\log p(x,t)}_{\text{no simplification}} + \underbrace{\frac{g^2(t)}{2} I(p)}_{\text{Diffusion contribution}}\\
&\ne \frac{\partial H}{\partial t}
\end{aligned}$$

## Appendix U

To compute the score function in the reverse Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE), group the linear term, and solve for the mean and variance, we consider a general forward SDE and derive the reverse SDE without assuming the forward and reverse processes are identical. The key steps involve leveraging the Fokker-Planck equation and properties of the score function.

### 1. **Forward and Reverse SDEs**
Consider the **forward SDE**:
$$
d\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t)  dt + g(t)  d\mathbf{w}
$$
where $\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t)$ is the drift, $g(t)$ is the diffusion coefficient, and $\mathbf{w}$ is a standard Wiener process.

The **reverse-time SDE** is given by:
$$
d\mathbf{x} = \left[ \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}) \right]  dt + g(t)  d\bar{\mathbf{w}}
$$
where $\bar{\mathbf{w}}$ is a reverse-time Wiener process, and $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$ is the **score function** of the marginal density $p_t(\mathbf{x})$.

### 2. **Score Function and Linear Drift Assumption**
The score $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x})$ is intractable in general. However, if the **forward drift is linear**:
$$
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \mathbf{A}(t) \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}(t)
$$
and the marginal distribution $p_t(\mathbf{x})$ is **Gaussian** (common in diffusion models), then:
$$
p_t(\mathbf{x}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}; \boldsymbol{\mu}(t), \boldsymbol{\Sigma}(t))
$$
The score function simplifies to:
$$
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p_t(\mathbf{x}) = -\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}(t))
$$

### 3. **Reverse SDE with Grouped Linear Term**
Substitute the score into the reverse SDE:
$$
d\mathbf{x} = \left[ \mathbf{A}(t) \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}(t) - g(t)^2 \left( -\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}(t)) \right) \right]  dt + g(t)  d\bar{\mathbf{w}}
$$
Simplify the drift:
$$
d\mathbf{x} = \left[ \mathbf{A}(t) \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}(t) + g(t)^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}(t)) \right]  dt + g(t)  d\bar{\mathbf{w}}
$$
Group linear and constant terms in $\mathbf{x}$:
$$
d\mathbf{x} = \left[ \underbrace{\left( \mathbf{A}(t) + g(t)^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) \right)}_{\text{Linear coefficient}} \mathbf{x} + \underbrace{\left( \mathbf{b}(t) - g(t)^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) \boldsymbol{\mu}(t) \right)}_{\text{Constant term}} \right]  dt + g(t)  d\bar{\mathbf{w}}
$$
Thus, the reverse drift is linear in $\mathbf{x}$.

### 4. **Solving for Mean and Variance**
Assume the reverse process is Gaussian (inherited from the forward process). Let $\boldsymbol{\mu}_r(t)$ and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r(t)$ be the mean and covariance of the reverse process. Since the reverse SDE is linear, the moments evolve as:
$$
\frac{d\boldsymbol{\mu}_r}{dt} = \left( \mathbf{A}(t) + g(t)^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) \right) \boldsymbol{\mu}_r + \mathbf{b}(t) - g(t)^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) \boldsymbol{\mu}(t)
$$
$$
\frac{d\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r}{dt} = \left( \mathbf{A}(t) + g(t)^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) \right) \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r + \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r \left( \mathbf{A}(t) + g(t)^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) \right)^\top + g(t)^2 \mathbf{I}

$$
\boldsymbol{\mu}_r(t) = \boldsymbol{\mu}(t), \quad \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r(t) = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}(t)

\frac{d\boldsymbol{\mu}}{dt} = \left( \mathbf{A}(t) + g(t)^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) \right) \boldsymbol{\mu} + \mathbf{b}(t) - g(t)^2 \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(t) \boldsymbol{\mu}

  \frac{d\boldsymbol{\mu}}{dt} = \mathbf{A}(t) \boldsymbol{\mu} + \mathbf{b}(t), \quad \frac{d\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}{dt} = \mathbf{A}(t) \boldsymbol{\Sigma} + \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{A}^\top(t) + g(t)^2 \mathbf{I}
  $$
  with initial conditions $\boldsymbol{\mu}(0), \boldsymbol{\Sigma}(0)$. The reverse process uses the same $\boldsymbol{\mu}(t), \boldsymbol{\Sigma}(t)$ via time reparameterization. This proves consistency without assuming forward-reverse symmetry.
