
# Summary

AI has become the central engine of semiconductor innovation. The first wave of latest AI era has been driven by foundation-model training and followed by rapid expansion of AI applications—content generation, AI search, AI coding, autonomous driving, and personal assistants—shifting the focus from training to inference, generating **quadrillions of tokens per month** across GPUs and ASICs in data centers, inference servers, and connected edge devices including AI PCs, smartphones, automotive, etc..

As AI workloads migrate outward from the cloud, computing evolves from **Generative → Agentic → Physical AI**, where models not only generate but also perceive, plan, and act in both virtual and physical worlds. Sustaining this expansion requires **scalable, energy-efficient architectures** spanning datacenters to edge devices. The challenge has shifted from transistor scaling to **system-level efficiency**—balancing performance, energy, bandwidth, and cost.

**Key technology vectors:** energy efficiency, compute scalability, high-bandwidth memory, high-speed communication, and thermal management. These technologies are essential across all computing segments, though each segment would face its own challenge and opportunity due to difference in form factor, computing demands, power supply, and cost constraint.

In the **cloud**, priorities include scaling compute with high-speed interconnects, HBM, and advanced power delivery. 


At the **edge**, the focus shifts to energy-efficient AI under tight form-factor and power constraints. **Thermal management** remains a shared priority across both domains.

From transistor to system, **GAA nanosheet transistors** extend Moore’s Law, while **chiplet-based heterogeneous integration** provides modular scalability. **Optical–electrical convergence** via co-packaged photonics enables multi-terabit chip-to-chip bandwidth within practical power budgets. **AI-native architectures**—supporting sparsity, quantization, and model-compatible formats—bridge evolving algorithms with silicon capabilities.

Looking ahead, AI demands **industry-wide innovation and collaboration**—from devices to architectures to software—to sustain exponential growth beyond conventional scaling. The next decade of semiconductor progress will be defined not by transistor density alone but by **energy-aware, model-compatible, and system-optimized designs** that make intelligent computing truly ubiquitous—from gigawatt-scale datacenters to watt-level edge devices.
