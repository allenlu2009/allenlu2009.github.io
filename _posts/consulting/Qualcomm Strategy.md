## Implications for Qualcomm’s Semiconductor & Compute Roadmap

- **From Mobile-Only to a Broad Semiconductor Portfolio:** Qualcomm’s business was historically centered on smartphone SoCs and modems—a market now mature and slower-growing, especially after losing Apple’s modem business. In 2025, Qualcomm began **aggressively expanding** into two major areas:  
	(1) **Data Center** 
	(2) **IoT** 
   
- **Cloud-to-Edge AI Everywhere Strategy:** Nearly all of Qualcomm’s 2025 moves tie into AI. The company aims to deliver AI compute from cloud to edge:
	- In the cloud/data center, it is re-entering the server CPU market with Oryon and Ventana cores and offering AI inference solutions via Cloud AI200/250—plus collaborating with Nvidia for GPU integration.
	- At the edge, Qualcomm is leveraging its on-device AI to gain share in XR, automotive, PCs, and IoT devices.
-
- **Qualcomm is leveraging their CPU to enter data center (AI) server market.**  The roadmap includes a high performance and energy-efficient server-class CPU by 2026–2027 using the **Nuvia CPU**, and possibly future integrates RISC-V cores or other IP from Ventana.  The hiring of a veteran Intel Xeon chief architect (Sailesh Kottapalli) in early 2025, plus Ventana’s team, gives Qualcomm a strong CPU engineering bench. With Alphawave’s interconnects, we might see **chiplet-based server CPUs** that can be tailored for different workloads (mixing CPU tiles, NICs, Cloud AI200/250 accelerator, etc.).  Qualcomm’s partnership with Nvidia’s NVLink Fusion further hints that its CPUs will be designed to easily pair with accelerators in large-scale deployments.
